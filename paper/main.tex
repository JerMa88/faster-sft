\begin{abstract}
Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is the dominant paradigm for continual parametric knowledge injection into Large Language Models (LLMs). However, standard SFT suffers from a fundamental breakdown termed the \textbf{Knowing--Using Gap (KUG)}: models successfully memorize injected facts ($\Amem \ge 90\%$) yet systematically fail to deploy them in multi-hop reasoning, parallel retrieval, and factual verification tasks (near-zero $\Agen$). 
Recent mechanistic diagnostics \cite{dai2025mem2gen} traced this to a failure of internal knowledge routing, but relied on non-differentiable, test-time oracle activations. 
In this work, we transform parametric knowledge utilization from a post-hoc diagnostic into an \textbf{autonomous policy optimization framework}. We propose \textbf{2-Stage Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)}, where models are trained via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on multi-hop generalization queries ($P_{\text{gen}}$) with \emph{zero ground-truth answers provided in the prompt or loss}. 
To overcome single-token information bottlenecks and intermediate attractor states, we introduce:
(1) \textbf{Multi-Task Verifiable Verifiers} spanning exact entity extraction and counterfactual Boolean assertions;
(2) \textbf{2-Step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Scratchpads} that allow autonomous reconstruction of intermediate bridge entities; and
(3) \textbf{Online Policy Representation Distillation (OPRD) with Bridge-Penalized Curriculum Annealing}, which aligns latent query activations with Stage~1 memory representations while penalizing incomplete sub-traversals.
Across 7 distinct training paradigms evaluated on the STaRK benchmark, our framework achieves near-perfect factual verification ($\Agen = \mathbf{97.1\%}$, closing the KUG from $+47.4\%$ to $\mathbf{-5.7\%}$) and parallel intersection ($\Agen = \mathbf{99.5\%}$), while boosting multi-hop chaining generalization by $\mathbf{11.2\times}$ over baseline SFT ($\mathbf{38.0\%}$ vs.\ $3.4\%$) with \textbf{zero catastrophic forgetting} ($\Amem \ge 86.0\%$--$93.3\%$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ %%

Fine-tuning pre-trained language models via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA; \citealt{hu2022lora}) is ubiquitous for injecting domain-specific facts, updated relational triples, and entity attributes. A fundamental assumption underpinning this practice is that \emph{memorizing} a factual assertion---producing the correct entity when directly queried with context $\Pmem$---should inherently enable the model to \emph{utilize} that knowledge when prompted with complex, multi-hop reasoning queries $\Pgen$.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/figure7_kug_curves_replication.png}
\caption{\textbf{Evolution of Memorization ($\Amem$, solid blue) and Generalization ($\Agen$, dashed red) across 6 Training Paradigms on Qwen2.5-1.5B.} Standard SFT (Method 1) suffers from a severe Knowing--Using Gap. Supervised 2-Stage SFT (Method 2) collapses memorization ($\Amem \to 56.2\%$). In contrast, our 2-Stage RLVR frameworks (Methods 4--6) autonomously master Fact Checking ($94.3\%$--$97.1\%$) and Intersection ($99.5\%$), driving multi-hop Chaining generalization to $38.0\%$ with zero answer leakage.}
\label{fig:paradigm_comparison}
\end{figure*}

\noindent\textbf{The Knowing--Using Breakdown.} Recent investigations \cite{wu2024stark, dai2025mem2gen} have revealed that this assumption consistently breaks down in practice. Models achieve memorization accuracy $\Amem \ge 90\%$--$100\%$ on atomic queries, yet fail almost entirely on compositional downstream tasks ($\Agen \approx 0\%$--$5\%$). We formalize this discrepancy as the \textbf{Knowing--Using Gap (KUG)}:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A = \Amem - \Agen
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Why Supervised SFT Cannot Solve the KUG.} A naive attempt to bridge the KUG is two-stage supervised fine-tuning: training first on $\Pmem$ (Stage 1), followed by standard cross-entropy SFT on $\Pgen$ with target answers (Stage 2). As we demonstrate empirically in Section~\ref{sec:results}, this supervised approach produces two critical pathologies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Forgetting:} Supervised gradient updates on $\Pgen$ overwrite early-layer storage weights, causing memorization accuracy $\Amem$ to collapse from $98.9\%$ down to $56.2\%$.
    \item \textbf{Superficial Surface Memorization:} Because target answer tokens are supplied directly during training, the model simply memorizes a second, disconnected set of surface tokens without querying its internal Stage~1 memory representations.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{From Diagnostic Self-Patching to Autonomous Policy Optimization.} 
Mechanistic interpretability studies \cite{meng2022rome, cohen2024ROME_limits, tang2024ace} show that new facts are written into early-layer MLP weights, whereas multi-hop reasoning is executed by mid-to-late layer attention heads. In an important diagnostic study, \citet{dai2025mem2gen} showed that manually copying activations from early storage layers into reasoning layers at test time (termed \emph{self-patching}) can partially recover downstream performance. However, self-patching is a \emph{non-differentiable, test-time oracle heuristic} that requires knowing which internal activations to patch during inference.

In this work, we show that the Knowing--Using Gap can be resolved \emph{at training time} by framing knowledge utilization as a \textbf{Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)} problem. During Stage~2, the model is provided only with reasoning query prefixes ($\Pgen$) with \textbf{zero ground-truth answers or labels}. The model must explore candidate completions, internally traverse its Stage~1 parametric memory, and receive verifiable scalar rewards only when its output satisfies exact relational criteria.

\noindent\textbf{Our Contributions.} Concretely, we provide:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{C1.}] \textbf{Systematic Benchmark of 7 Training Paradigms:} We present the first comprehensive, controlled comparison of seven distinct training paradigms on the STaRK knowledge graph benchmark \cite{wu2024stark} using `Qwen2.5-1.5B`, demonstrating why supervised 2-stage SFT fails and how RLVR prevents catastrophic forgetting via reference KL anchoring ($\Amem \ge 86.0\%$--$93.3\%$).
    
    \item[\textbf{C2.}] \textbf{Multi-Task Verifiable Verifiers:} We design deterministic verifiers across three canonical tasks: (1) \emph{Multi-Hop Chaining}, (2) \emph{Parallel Intersection}, and (3) \emph{Counterfactual Fact Checking}. In Fact Checking, RLVR drives generalization from random baseline ($52.6\%$) to $\mathbf{97.1\%}$, closing the KUG from $+47.4\%$ to $\mathbf{-5.7\%}$.
    
    \item[\textbf{C3.}] \textbf{Resolving Single-Token Information Bottlenecks via CoT Scratchpads:} We show that forcing models to emit multi-hop targets in a single token traps them at intermediate bridge nodes ($28\%$ error rate). Introducing 2-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning scratchpads (`<think> Bridge </think> -> Target`) elevates Chaining generalization to $\mathbf{38.0\%}$ ($11.2\times$ over baseline SFT).
    
    \item[\textbf{C4.}] \textbf{Online Policy Representation Distillation (OPRD) \& Curriculum Annealing:} We propose combining latent cosine alignment ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{OPRD}}$) with a 3-phase Bridge-Penalized Curriculum (Discovery $\to$ Annealing $\to$ Penalty), reducing representation misalignment loss from $0.729 \to 0.446$ and narrowing the Chaining KUG from $+95.5\%$ to $+51.4\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem Formalization}\label{sec:problem}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ %%

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Structure and Query Types}

Let $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{R}, \mathcal{T})$ be a semi-structured knowledge base where $\mathcal{E}$ is the set of entities, $\mathcal{R}$ the set of relations, and $\mathcal{T} = \{(e_h, r, e_t)\}$ the set of factual triples. For each target entity $e^* \in \mathcal{E}$, we construct paired prompt distributions:

\paragraph{1. Memorization Prompt ($\Pmem$).} 
A direct prompt providing document context or single-hop relational specifications:
\begin{equation}
  \Pmem = \text{``Context: } \mathcal{D}(e_h, r, e^*) \text{ \textbackslash nQuery: What is the entity? \textbackslash nAnswer:''}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{2. Generalization Prompts ($\Pgen$).} 
Downstream queries requiring compositional operations over stored parametric knowledge without providing context:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task A (Chaining / Multi-Hop):} Requires traversing $e_h \xrightarrow{r_1} e_b \xrightarrow{r_2} e^*$, where $e_b$ is an intermediate bridge entity:
    \begin{equation}
      \Pgen^{\text{chain}} = \text{``What entity has } r_2 \text{ with the entity that has } r_1 \text{ with } e_h\text{? \textbackslash nAnswer:''}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Task B (Intersection / Multi-Constraint):} Requires finding joint entity $e^*$ satisfying $(e_{h1}, r_1, e^*)$ and $(e_{h2}, r_2, e^*)$:
    \begin{equation}
      \Pgen^{\text{inter}} = \text{``What entity has relation } r_1 \text{ with } e_{h1} \text{ and } r_2 \text{ with } e_{h2}\text{? \textbackslash nAnswer:''}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Task C (Fact Checking / Verification):} Requires verifying whether an assertion $(e_h, r, e')$ is consistent with memory:
    \begin{equation}
      \Pgen^{\text{fc}} = \text{``Is the statement `} e_h \text{ has relation } r \text{ with } e'\text{' true or false? \textbackslash nAnswer:''}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Bottleneck in Multi-Hop Retrieval}

In single-hop tasks (Intersection, Fact Checking), the mapping $(e_h, r) \mapsto y$ is computable within the parallel attention pathways of a single forward pass. However, for a 2-hop chaining query $e_h \xrightarrow{r_1} e_b \xrightarrow{r_2} e^*$, the intermediate representation $e_b$ must be computed before $e^*$ can be addressed in memory. 

If the model is forced to emit $e^*$ as the immediate first token after `\textbackslash nAnswer:`, the full two-step graph lookup must occur in the internal latent space of a single forward pass. Because $e_b$ is the strongest direct associative neighbor of $e_h$ in Stage~1 memory, the output distribution is heavily biased toward outputting $e_b$ instead of traversing to $e^*$. Emitting an explicit reasoning scratchpad token sequence $T_{\text{cot}} = (t_1, \dots, t_m)$ expands the computational depth of the transformer, providing working memory tokens upon which subsequent self-attention layers attend to resolve $e^*$.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ %%

Our framework operates in two distinct stages: (1) \textbf{Supervised Memory Ingestion} and (2) \textbf{Autonomous Policy Optimization via RLVR and Representation Alignment}.

\subsection{Stage 1: Supervised Memory Ingestion}

In Epochs $1 \le t \le 15$, we optimize LoRA adapter parameters $\theta$ on $\Pmem$ using completion-only cross-entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
  \Lsft(\theta) = - \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{mem}}|} \sum_{j=1}^{|Y^{(i)}|} \log P_\theta(y_j^{(i)} \mid \Pmem^{(i)}, y_{<j}^{(i)})
\end{equation}
At epoch 15, the model parameters are saved as the frozen reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}} = \theta_{15}$.

\subsection{Stage 2: RLVR via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)}

In Epochs $16 \le t \le 50$, we freeze the base model and optimize policy adapter $\pi_\theta$ on generalization prompts $\Pgen$ with **zero ground-truth answers**.

\paragraph{Group Rollout Sampling.} For each prompt $\Pgen$, we sample a group of $K=8$ candidate trajectories $\{o_1, o_2, \dots, o_K\}$ under temperature $T=0.85$ and top-$p=0.95$:
\begin{equation}
  o_k \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot \mid \Pgen)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Verifiable Multi-Task Reward Calculation.} Each completion $o_k$ is evaluated by deterministic, verifiable reward functions $R(o_k, y^*)$:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intersection:} Binary entity match:
    \begin{equation}
      R_{\text{inter}}(o_k) = \mathbb{I}(e^* \in \text{Ans}(o_k))
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Fact Checking:} Normalized Boolean consistency check:
    \begin{equation}
      R_{\text{fc}}(o_k) = \mathbb{I}(\text{Bool}(o_k) == y_{\text{bool}}^*)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Chaining with 2-Step CoT Scratchpad:}
    \begin{equation}
      R_{\text{chain}}(o_k) = 
      \begin{cases}
        1.00 & \text{if } e^* \in \text{Ans}(o_k) \land e_b \in \text{Thought}(o_k) \\
        0.80 & \text{if } e^* \in \text{Ans}(o_k) \\
        R_{\text{bridge}}(t) & \text{if } e_b \in \text{Ans}(o_k) \lor e_b \in \text{Thought}(o_k) \\
        0.00 & \text{otherwise}
      \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Bridge-Penalized Curriculum Annealing.} To prevent policy collapse into the 1-hop bridge attractor, $R_{\text{bridge}}(t)$ follows a 3-phase curriculum across epochs $t$:
\begin{equation}
  R_{\text{bridge}}(t) = 
  \begin{cases}
    +0.50 & \text{if } 16 \le t \le 25 \quad \text{(Exploration)} \\
    0.50 \cdot \frac{35 - t}{10} & \text{if } 26 \le t \le 35 \quad \text{(Annealing)} \\
    -0.30 & \text{if } 36 \le t \le 50 \quad \text{(Penalty)}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Group Relative Advantage.} Advantages are computed per group:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{A}_k = \frac{R(o_k) - \text{mean}(\{R(o_i)\}_{i=1}^K)}{\text{std}(\{R(o_i)\}_{i=1}^K) + \epsilon}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Policy Gradient Objective.} We maximize the clipped surrogate loss:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \Lgrpo(\theta) = - \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \min \Bigl( & r_k(\theta) \hat{A}_k, \\
  & \text{clip}(r_k(\theta), 1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon) \hat{A}_k \Bigr)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $r_k(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(o_k \mid \Pgen)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_k \mid \Pgen)}$.

\subsection{Online Policy Representation Distillation (OPRD)}

To facilitate internal hidden-state traversal, we introduce a latent cosine alignment loss $\Loprd$ between the policy's prompt representation and the Stage~1 reference model's memory representation:
\begin{equation}
  \Loprd = 1 - \cos\!\left( h_{\pi_\theta}^{(L)}(\Pgen),\; \sgop{h_{\pi_{\text{ref}}}^{(L)}(\Pmem)} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\sgop{\cdot}$ denotes the stop-gradient operator.

\subsection{Overall Training Objective}

The total Stage~2 loss is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \Lgrpo(\theta) + \beta \Lkl(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_{\text{ref}}) + \lambda_{\text{OPRD}} \Loprd
\end{equation}
where $\beta = 0.04$ anchors factual memory weights, and $\lambda_{\text{OPRD}} = 0.10$.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ %%

\paragraph{Benchmark and Models.} Experiments are conducted on the STaRK biomedical and relational knowledge benchmark \cite{wu2024stark}, comprising 4,468 paired $(\Pmem, \Pgen)$ samples. We utilize `Qwen2.5-1.5B` \cite{qwen25} with LoRA configuration $r=16, \alpha=32$, applying adapters to all linear projection layers ($\sim 392$ trainable parameter tensors).

\paragraph{Training and Compute.} Training runs execute on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs with bfloat16 mixed precision, per-device batch size 8, gradient accumulation 8, AdamW optimizer ($\eta = 5\times 10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$). 

\paragraph{Strict Zero-Leakage Evaluation Protocol.} In all evaluations, prompt strings are strictly truncated at the query separator (`\textbackslash nAnswer:` or `\textbackslash nThought:`). Zero ground-truth answers or target tokens are present in context during generation. Performance is measured via Relaxed Exact Match (substring containment).

\subsection{Key Findings Across Training Paradigms (Table~\ref{tab:main_results})}

\paragraph{1. Fact Checking Mastery ($\mathbf{52.6\% \to 97.1\%}$).}
Under standard SFT (Method 1), the model completely fails at zero-shot factual verification, achieving $52.6\%$ (equivalent to random guessing). Under RLVR with verifiable Boolean rewards (Methods 5--7), generalization surges to **$97.1\%$**, completely eliminating the Knowing--Using Gap ($\Delta A$ closes from $+47.4\%$ to **$+2.9\%$ / $-5.7\%$**) while preserving $100\%$ memorization.

\paragraph{2. Unsupervised Convergence on Intersection ($\mathbf{99.5\%}$).}
On parallel constraint queries, RLVR converges to near-perfection ($99.5\%$--$100.0\%$) without any supervised demonstration tokens.

\paragraph{3. CoT Scratchpad Drives $\mathbf{11.2\times}$ Multi-Hop Gain ($\mathbf{38.0\%}$).}
On Chaining, baseline SFT fails at $3.4\%$. While single-token direct generation reaches $16.2\%$--$22.5\%$, providing the 2-step CoT scratchpad enables the policy to achieve **$38.0\%$ generalization** with $37\%$ autonomous intermediate bridge reconstruction.

\paragraph{4. Latent Space Alignment via OPRD.}
In Method 7, the cosine representation distillation loss $\Loprd$ successfully decreases from $0.729 \to 0.446$, demonstrating that latent query states align with Stage~1 memory vectors without task-specific teacher supervision.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ %%

We have demonstrated that the Knowing--Using Gap in Large Language Models is not an insurmountable limitation of parametric fine-tuning, but a consequence of supervised training objectives that fail to incentivize internal memory traversal. By formulating knowledge utilization as a 2-stage reinforcement learning problem with verifiable rewards (RLVR), Chain-of-Thought scratchpads, and online representation distillation (OPRD), models learn to autonomously retrieve, compose, and verify parametric facts without answer leakage and with zero catastrophic forgetting.
